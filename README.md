
# Controlled SUDEP Risk Factor Vocabulary (CSRV)

The **Controlled SUDEP Risk Factor Vocabulary (CSRV)** is a structured biomedical vocabulary for representing risk factors associated with **Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy (SUDEP)**.

CSRV is being developed to provide a standardized representation of SUDEP risk factors and their clinically relevant characteristics. The vocabulary is intended to support consistent annotation, information extraction, cohort identification, and computational analysis of SUDEP risk information in clinical and research data.

## Background

Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy (SUDEP) is a major cause of premature mortality among people with epilepsy. A range of clinical, seizure-related, treatment-related, demographic, and behavioral factors have been associated with SUDEP risk. However, information describing these factors may be represented inconsistently across clinical documentation, research datasets, risk-assessment instruments, and biomedical terminologies.

This variability creates challenges for:

- consistently representing SUDEP risk factors;
- identifying risk-factor information in clinical narratives;
- integrating information across heterogeneous datasets;
- conducting reproducible cohort queries and retrospective studies; and
- developing automated SUDEP risk-assessment methods.

CSRV addresses this problem by providing a controlled, machine-readable representation of SUDEP risk-factor concepts.

## Purpose

The primary goal of CSRV is to establish a common semantic representation of SUDEP risk factors that can support both human interpretation and computational processing.

The vocabulary is designed to support applications such as:

- **clinical NLP** – identifying mentions of SUDEP risk factors in clinical text;
- **information extraction** – normalizing extracted risk-factor mentions to controlled concepts;
- **clinical annotation** – providing standardized concepts for manual and automated annotation;
- **cohort identification** – supporting structured queries for patients with particular SUDEP risk factors;
- **risk assessment** – providing a semantic foundation for automated SUDEP risk-factor identification and assessment;
- **data integration** – improving interoperability across clinical, research, and epidemiological datasets; and
- **research reproducibility** – providing explicit definitions and identifiers for concepts used in SUDEP studies.
